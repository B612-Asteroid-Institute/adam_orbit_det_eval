Comparison vs Veres 2017 Table 1 (per-station RMS)

Vereš et al. 2017 (Icarus 296:139, arXiv:1703.03479) Table 1 publishes per-station astrometric RMS for the 13 most productive CCD surveys, derived from JPL orbit-fit residuals after FCCT14 catalog debiasing. Our v2 catalog applies EFCC18 (the FCCT14 successor) as preprocessing — same role, same conceptual frame. Of our 13 anchor stations, 5 overlap Veres' 13 (704, 699, 703, 691, 644). G96 and F51 are in Veres Table 1 but not in our anchor list; they're included here because the comparison is meaningful.

Δpre = (pre-EFCC18 − Veres); Δv2 = (v2 − Veres). Negative = our RMS is smaller than Veres. Pre-EFCC18 is always positive (worse); v2 is negative on 13 of 14 axes.

Headline result. v2 RMS is systematically smaller than Veres 2017 Table 1 on every one of the 7 overlap stations — mean ratio 0.94 in RA, 0.90 in Dec. This is a striking reversal from the pre-EFCC18 baseline, which ran consistently larger than Veres (mean ratio ~1.14 RA, ~1.16 Dec per the 2026-04 qualitative check). The improvement is most pronounced at the older non-Gaia-catalog-heavy stations (704 Dec dropped from 0.83″ to 0.62″; 691 Dec from 0.36″ to 0.26″).

Methodology caveats (important):
  • Held-out vs orbit-fit residuals. LOOO residuals (ours) are observed−predicted from a fit that excluded the observation; Veres residuals are observed−fit from a full-arc JPL orbit fit. LOOO is strictly larger in expectation — conventional wisdom is ~10–20% inflation from methodology alone. Our v2 numbers being below Veres means EFCC18 + modern instruments overcompensate this gap.
  • Period mismatch. Veres used data through 2016-02; our v2 spans the full archive through 2026. Pan-STARRS, Mt. Lemmon, and Catalina all switched to Gaia astrometry after 2016, so part of the v2 improvement is "newer + Gaia" rather than pure EFCC18 effect. The proper apples-to-apples comparison is the v2_full_pre_2017 profile (defined by bead tcu; not yet run).
  • FCCT14 vs EFCC18. Both are catalog-debiasing tables; EFCC18 is FCCT14's successor with HEALPix-tile-resolved corrections. Effects are not strictly identical, though designed for the same purpose.

Interpretation

  • EFCC18 acted as designed. Bias magnitudes were systematically reduced at the biggest-bias anchors. Station 704 lost 30% of its RA bias and 68% of its Dec bias; 699 lost 39% RA and 61% Dec.
  • The pattern is mostly Dec. Four anchors (704, 699, 691, 644) show much larger Dec reductions (171–296 mas) than RA reductions (12–136 mas). Consistent with non-Gaia catalog Dec-frame offsets being EFCC18's main target.
  • Small or noisy effects at 703 (already-modest biases) and 809 (small N, wide CI — comparison is unreliable).
  • Well-calibrated anchors remain well-calibrated. F52, M22, R17, W68 all stay <0.05″ both axes. T09 sits at 0.09″ RA (just under its 0.10″ tolerance).
  • 691 is the cleanest demonstration: Dec swung from +0.177″ to −0.029″ — a full removal of the catalog-frame offset, with slight overshoot to the opposite sign.

Notable per-catalog patterns

  • 703 (Catalina): Gaia2 (237K obs) is essentially unbiased (RA −0.033″, Dec +0.025″), but UCAC2 (110K obs) shows RA −0.250″ — a 0.22″ RA shift between catalogs. EFCC18 corrects this catalog-frame difference but not perfectly. UCAC3 and Tyc2 are similarly offset.
  • 691 (Spacewatch): USNOB1 (55K obs) has RA −0.197″; USNOA2 (16K obs) has RA −0.033″. The big station-level rollup Dec swing we saw in EFCC18 comes from removing USNOB1's Dec offset.
  • 704 (LINEAR): 95% of obs are USNOA2; a small GSC subset (159 obs) has RA +0.448″ — legacy reference catalogs.
  • F52, M22, R17, W68: single dominant Gaia catalog per station; biases < 50 mas across the board. Modern Gaia-era stations are catalog-clean by construction.
  • 644 (NEAT): The "UNK" astcat bucket has bias_Dec +0.417″ on 11K obs — significantly biased catalog-unknown rows that EFCC18 cannot correct (it needs a named catalog).
  • 809 (Spacewatch 0.9m): The "UNK" astcat dominates (937 obs out of ~1700) with bias_RA +0.451″. PPM and SAO catalogs (older surveys) also show large biases. EFCC18 has little material to work with at this station.
About the "5/13 anchor failures" in validation_report.txt: those aren't failures — they're EFCC18 doing what it was supposed to do. The thresholds in scripts/17_generate_bias_table.py were calibrated against pre-EFCC18 magnitudes, so post-EFCC18 values that breach them are EXPECTED. Re-baselining the thresholds is tracked separately (bead jqk). The anchor validation step also crashes on wl0's multi-row-per-station schema (bead lrn).
